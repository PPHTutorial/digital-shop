Building Trust With Testimonials When You Only Have Three Customers

0

New sellers often wait to display any testimonials at all until they have accumulated what feels like a respectable number, ten, twenty, fifty, which leaves a genuinely new store looking untested and unproven for months longer than necessary, when even three honest testimonials, used well, can meaningfully shift a hesitant visitor's decision.

Three specific testimonials beat thirty vague ones

A generic testimonial, "great product, highly recommend," provides almost no real evidence to a skeptical visitor regardless of how many similar ones are displayed together. A single testimonial describing a specific situation, a specific result, and specific details about how the product was used carries far more persuasive weight, even alone, than a wall of generic five-star quotes with no real substance behind them.

Getting a real testimonial out of an early customer

Most customers, asked simply "would you leave a review," respond with something generic because they have not been prompted to think about specifics. Asking a more specific question instead, "what was the exact result you got from using this, and what almost stopped you from buying it," produces a genuinely more detailed, more useful, and more persuasive response than a generic request ever does.

Where to display a small handful of testimonials for maximum effect

  • Near the top of the product page, close to the initial pitch, rather than buried at the very bottom where only the most committed readers will see them.
  • Placed right next to the specific claim or feature they support, so the testimonial functions as direct evidence for a particular point rather than generic decoration.
  • Repeated, where relevant, in your welcome email sequence and in follow up marketing, since a small number of strong testimonials can be reused across many contexts rather than needing constant new ones.

Being honest about a small sample size

There is no need to imply a testimonial represents dozens of similar happy customers when it represents three. Being transparent, even mentioning that you are a newer seller building a track record, paired with genuinely strong, specific evidence from those first few customers, reads as more credible to a discerning buyer than vague language implying a scale that does not yet exist.

What to do while waiting for more reviews to accumulate

Beyond direct testimonials, other forms of early trust building help fill the gap: a clear, generous refund policy that reduces the buyer's risk regardless of social proof volume, transparency about your process and background, and visible responsiveness to any public questions or comments, all signal trustworthiness independently of testimonial count.

The compounding value of getting this right early

A new store that uses its first three genuine, specific testimonials well, placed prominently and honestly framed, converts meaningfully better in its early months than a store that hides behind having "not enough reviews yet" and waits passively for volume to accumulate on its own. Every one of those genuinely well-used early testimonials also makes the next real review easier to earn, since satisfied customers respond well to seeing that previous feedback was taken seriously and displayed prominently rather than lost in a generic review section nobody actually reads.

Video testimonials carry disproportionate weight even in small numbers

A single short, genuine video testimonial, even filmed casually on a phone by an early customer willing to share one, tends to build more trust than several written quotes, since a real person's voice and face are harder to fake and easier for a skeptical visitor to relate to than text alone. Asking one particularly satisfied early customer specifically for a short video, rather than assuming nobody would be willing, is worth the modest awkwardness of the ask, given how disproportionately persuasive even one good video testimonial can be relative to a written quote covering the same underlying story.

Do not let a small number of testimonials go stale

Continuing to display the exact same three testimonials a year later, long after dozens of newer customers have had equally good experiences, signals to an attentive visitor that growth may have stalled since those early days, even if that is not actually true. Periodically rotating in newer testimonials as they arrive, while retiring older ones or moving them to a secondary section, keeps a storefront's social proof looking current and active rather than frozen at an early, less established stage of the business.
